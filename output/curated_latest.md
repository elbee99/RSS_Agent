## 1. [\<i\>Operando\</i\> Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry Reveals Iodine-Mediated Redox Chemistry in Solid-State Li–S Batteries](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02036)

**8/10** · *ACS Energy Letters* · operando ToF-SIMS

A dynamic operando ToF-SIMS platform follows a solid-state Li–S cathode under bias. It detects a rising I2– signal on charging in LiI-containing cathodes, which indicates elemental iodine formation. Sulfur ion signals change at the same time, pointing to iodine-mediated redox. The work detects transient, low-concentration species at buried interfaces that other operando methods miss.

## 2. [Quantitative Measurement of Li‐Ion Concentration in NCM811 Cathodes via Optimized Electrochemical Strain Microscopy](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/smtd.71201?af=R)

**6/10** · *Small Methods* · Electrochemical strain microscopy (AFM-based) with ICP-MS calibration

Turns ESM into a calibrated Li-content probe for NCM811: a stiff cantilever suppresses electrostatic and contact-potential artifacts, and faster acquisition allows multi-particle statistics. Pixel-averaged amplitude is calibrated against ICP-MS Li content, and three SOCs are classified with over 90% accuracy from four images. This is ex situ and gives only indirect quantification, but it is a useful methodological step.

## 3. [Layer Stacking Structure Governed Transition‐Metal Interlayer Migration for Reversible High‐Voltage Oxygen Redox Plateau](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.3309393?af=R)

**6/10** · *Angewandte Chemie International Edition* · ex situ/in situ XRD, STEM, RIXS/DFT (likely)

Compares P2 and P3 Na0.6Li0.2Mn0.6Ti0.2O2 with the same composition. P3 stacking suppresses O-type stacking faults, which hinders interlayer TM migration and molecular O2 formation, so the high-voltage oxygen plateau stays reversible. P2 transforms to O2 stacking and loses the plateau. This is a useful structure–mechanism link, but the evidence seems to be mostly conventional diffraction, microscopy and DFT.
